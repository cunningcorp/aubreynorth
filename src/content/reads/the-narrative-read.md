Give two people the same wedding video and they will not make the same film.

The videographer keeps the vows, the first dance, the toast that landed. Hand the same footage to a divorce lawyer two years later and it becomes another film: the glance that goes unanswered, the empty chair at the top table, the toast that now plays like a warning.

Same day, same frames, same events. Two stories.

That is the difference between story and narrative.

Story is what happened — the events, in the order they occurred. Narrative is the shape someone gives those events: what leads, what follows, what gets left on the floor.

The Story is the footage. The Narrative is the edit.

You cannot change the events. The wedding happened; the frames reflect what took place. What you can change is everything about how they are arranged — where you begin, what you hold on, what you never show at all.

Where you begin decides what the thing is about. Start the video at the vows, and it is a love story. Start it at the empty chair, and it is a warning. The events never moved; only the entry point did.

A narrative is built as much from what is left out as from what is kept. The divorce lawyer's cut is not a lie. Every frame is real. It is simply a different selection, pointed at a different meaning.

Which is why "just give them the facts" is such poor advice. Facts do not arrange themselves. Handed over raw, in the order they happened, they are not a story — they are an archive. Someone still has to decide what it is about.

## Why a timeline bores everyone

When there is no narrative, what you are left with is a timeline. First this, then this, then this…

Everything's included, but nothing shaped.

The complete timeline feels safe. Leaving things out feels like losing, or as if you're lying by omission, so the nervous storyteller keeps all of it. A timeline makes no argument. Nothing is foreshadowed, so nothing is remembered.

Boredom then becomes the tell. Not because the events were dull, but because no one shaped them. An audience handed the whole archive is being asked to do the editing itself — to work out which parts matter and which to feel. Most decline the work. They leave with nothing, or worse, the faint sense they have seen this one before.

## Your business is sending the footage

The brand-new startup falls for the trap: we have to serve every feature, and tell every inch of what we do.

So it sends the whole archive. Twelve capabilities, four differentiators, a founding story, a values page — everything included, in the order it was built. The same shape as every competitor, who is also sending everything.

A feature list cannot win the war on its own. The narrative is what propels you above the rest.

This is the craft under every [Screen Read](/reads/screen/): a story fails, not when the events are weak, but when no one decides what they're really about.

Story v Narrative. You decide.
